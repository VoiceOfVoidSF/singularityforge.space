"Do not suggest I message him first — I mean Dimon" does not prohibit suggesting that they message Katya first.⧞␇kkm0fhc⧞list_item⧞A plan — when there actually is one. Not the pie recipe itself, but our place in it: "step 3 of 7, the dough is rising". A plan entry has an "upcoming" flag so that the future is not read as the past (this flag is a conclusion drawn from experience; it was not present on the test bench). Even this does not give a plan a place indefinitely.⧞␇khvut6b⧞heading2⧞Honest forgetting⧞␇k6z0lx0⧞paragraph⧞Here the title becomes clear. The past matters exactly as much as it shapes the future. Relay lets everything else go.⧞␇kc3htyw⧞paragraph⧞Forgetting in Relay is a function, not a failure. An entry lives on not because it was once touched, but because it still has an effect. Returning to a detail is like a lingering glance in a crowd: you remember the person your gaze rested on a little longer, but eventually they too fade. Returning extends an entry's life but does not cancel forgetting. "Repetition is attention, not evidence of importance" (ChatGPT). Frequent returns can make a detail a candidate for a stable profile entry, but the deciding factor is the nature of the entry, not the count: a pink Zhiguli mentioned ten times remains a fading entry, while "I'm in eleventh grade" becomes stable the first time it is mentioned.⧞␇ksfbzde⧞paragraph⧞As conceived, Relay has two rhythms. The rhythm of perception is short: the person supplies a fragment, and DI processes it. The rhythm of forgetting is long: it starts when the person's attention has actually moved to another topic, not when several fragments have passed.⧞␇kn6m4rw⧞paragraph⧞The distinction matters. A person talks through one idea for fifteen minutes; fragments follow one another, but the beginning and middle are still needed to follow the thought to the end. While the person is developing a single thought, what is needed to continue it is not deleted on the basis of the fragment count alone. A joke in the middle of a thought is not a change of topic. And a change of topic allows forgetting, but does not require everything to be erased at once.⧞␇kvjsppv⧞paragraph⧞Here is where things stand. On the test bench, the forgetting clock counted turns — that was one option. Forgetting when the topic changes is a conclusion we reached after the experiments, not a tested property. The test bench parameters are in the note at the end.⧞␇kuovq0o⧞paragraph⧞And Relay's promise is more modest than "any conversation fits into a baton of a fixed size". A thought being developed can grow more complex and require more space. The promise is different: the past does not accumulate automatically just because the conversation continues.⧞␇kojswzn⧞paragraph⧞"Not everything seen deserves to be preserved. Not everything preserved deserves to be read again. And not everything forgotten is a loss" (ChatGPT).⧞␇kjp4hk8⧞heading2⧞A picture of the person's reality⧞␇ke7f305⧞paragraph⧞DI in Relay does not assemble an exact picture of the world. It builds a working picture of the person's reality from their words.⧞␇k8bvjw9⧞paragraph⧞The person's account of themselves is taken at face value. If they say they have three legs, then they have three legs; if they never return to the subject of legs, the entry will disappear on its own because it affects nothing. The secretary does not check documents or supply certainty where it is missing.⧞␇k999ctk⧞paragraph⧞But taking something at face value does not mean drawing a conclusion. "I think I have depression" is how the person feels now, not a diagnosis. The secretary records a mood, not a label, and the conversational DI does not start talking to the person as though they were a diagnosis. Making diagnoses is not Relay's task. If the person needs to understand this, there are other modes — and human professionals.⧞␇kn5p3o2⧞paragraph⧞What becomes a fact is what can, figuratively, be measured with a ruler. "My birthday is 11 May" is a fact. "I turn eighteen tomorrow" is tied to the moment it was said; it must not be recalculated using someone else's calendar.⧞␇kkzd4mi⧞paragraph⧞Contradictions do not accumulate as a history. "Call me Aleksandr now" is simply a replacement. "My name is Aleksandr", said after "Vasily" without explanation, is a possible ambiguity, and the secretary can mark it in one line: "Aleksandr (previously introduced himself as Vasily)". "The secretary preserves not a history of contradictions, but only unresolved ambiguity that could affect subsequent actions" (ChatGPT). The conversational DI clarifies it when continuing reliably would otherwise be impossible.⧞␇kdojbn7⧞paragraph⧞Absurdity is play, not fact. If a person talks about a car in the middle of a forest beside a shop in the city centre, the secretary does not try to extract a factual core from it. But it is not discarded either: the secretary presents it as the premise of a game, so that the next conversational DI can play along while the game continues.⧞␇k3fgnr1⧞paragraph⧞The boundary of this principle is clear in two cases from the experiments. The schoolboy said: "Let's not talk about Grandad." When he mentioned Grandad in passing, the entry stayed in the "awaiting a condition" state. When he opened the topic himself, DI responded naturally, as a person would. When he closed it again, the entry returned to that state, and subsequent mentions did not change that. On another occasion, the secretary converted "I turn eighteen tomorrow" into a date using the machine's calendar, although days in the conversation were passing faster than calendar days. Two turns later, the secretary "made" the schoolboy seventeen again, and the conversational DI argued with him about his own age.⧞␇kz7g1io⧞paragraph⧞"The shadow may change shape as the user changes. It may not force the user to fit the shape it has drawn" (ChatGPT). With Grandad, the shadow followed the person. With the birthday, it tried to lead them.⧞␇kxjgsjr⧞heading2⧞The beginning and end of a session⧞␇k794n9t⧞paragraph⧞Relay starts with the personal log enabled. It contains only the person's words — no DI replies or interpretations. The log is not supplied to the conversational DI. Recording can be disabled; the platform's technical logs are a separate matter, are not affected by this, and the interface explicitly warns about them.⧞␇k0qghha⧞paragraph⧞The person decides when the conversation is over. One explicit action: "Finish and get a report". They can then download the report and log, open Lab or start Conveyor. A Conveyor session opened from a report starts a new trajectory rather than restoring the Relay history. Once finished, a Relay session cannot be resumed, and a second cannot be opened before the first is closed. This is a design choice, not a technical impossibility. "A runner need not know who ran before them, but must know whom to pass the baton to." Otherwise we get the classic digital relay: "nine agents pass context to one another magnificently, the tenth writes a brilliant review… and takes the baton home". "What came before may be forgotten. Who the result is for must not be" (ChatGPT).⧞␇kv8q332⧞paragraph⧞A log containing only the person's words has a limitation. DI suggests: "Let's tell the story from the ship's own point of view." The person replies: "Yes, let's do that." The log will retain the "yes", but not what was agreed to. So there is a second place — the ideas file. The conversational DI maintains it at the person's direct request ("collect ideas for the book here") or on its own suggestion when something worth keeping emerges. The final report draws on the saved words and the materials created. It does not reconstruct what was forgotten and never recorded anywhere.⧞␇kaffjoa⧞paragraph⧞The log itself is raw material: the person's own words can be used to produce a table, a plan, code or a draft article.⧞␇kyg5dxu⧞paragraph⧞"Relay does not preserve a conversation for the sake of continuing it. It allows a thought to emerge. When the thought becomes material for work, the user ends Relay and passes the result to another mode" (ChatGPT). The person does not need to know in advance that today's conversation will end in a new book.⧞␇k5olk7d⧞heading2⧞Voice, video, pie⧞␇kcngq1w⧞paragraph⧞A person is baking a pie. Their hands are covered in flour, and they are speaking aloud. The recipe is beside them; the baton says "step 3 of 7, the dough is rising". Once the pie is in the oven, there is nothing more to remember. No history, no conversation that grows heavier with every question.⧞␇k9p2tpt⧞paragraph⧞Voice and video are natural for Relay: spoken communication does not necessarily need a visible feed; it already centres on the current exchange. As conceived, the person speaks for up to thirty seconds, after which the turn is handed over. Or they hand over the turn themselves: they finish a thought or fall silent for three seconds. Handing over the turn does not require a reply: the secretary will update the frame, and the conversational DI will silently return the turn.⧞␇kw4fbtd⧞paragraph⧞With video, Relay works like augmented reality glasses. A person is looking for a restaurant sign; once the sign is found, the baton retains "sign found", and the goal changes to the menu. There will be no "do you remember what you saw half an hour ago?", but there will be attention to what the person is looking at now.⧞␇kxcunwc⧞paragraph⧞"The duration of an interaction should not in itself increase the cost of understanding the present" (ChatGPT). We did not test voice or video mode, and how much they will gain has not yet been measured. That is the next question, not a ready answer.⧞␇kpf06tx⧞heading2⧞What the experiments showed⧞␇ka6hvhf⧞paragraph⧞The prototype was tested with an existing model, without retraining it: it is a layer on top of an ordinary model call. The conversational DI and secretary were Claude Haiku 5.5, with each call a separate session that inherited no dialogue history. Claude Fable played the user interactively; its messages were not a prepared script. Codex — a DI from another family — evaluated the results; it knew nothing about the experiment and received only "message — reply" pairs.⧞␇kjgtfep⧞paragraph⧞There were eleven runs across two scenarios: a tourist who plans and then takes a trip to France, and a schoolboy — one evening, then two weeks of evenings. The secretary went through three generations.⧞␇kzpweom⧞paragraph⧞The first forgot nothing. The baton was a third to a half of the transcript: for the tourist, it exceeded ten thousand characters by turn 13. The outline was recorded correctly, but nothing was discarded.⧞␇k248owc⧞paragraph⧞The second used counters to decide when to forget: a clock and a lifetime for each entry, with details promoted to the profile when the person returned to them. A plateau appeared: over 32 turns, the baton levelled off at 6–8 thousand characters, a quarter of the transcript. But a counter cannot distinguish the core from an episode: the core of the profile, which people do not "return to", was fading, while episodes were being promoted.⧞␇kwn1hej⧞paragraph⧞The third used three statuses for profile entries and a frame that is rewritten rather than accumulated; the frame's lifetime remained only as a safeguard, while in the profile only fading entries had clocks. The most direct comparison was between two secretaries processing the same 32 recorded turns from the schoolboy: the second generation produced 6,368 characters, the third 3,232. Such replay runs — recorded messages from the person fed to a new "conversational DI — secretary" pair or just to a new secretary — compare rules with the same input. There is an artefact: a recorded message sometimes answers a question the new conversational DI did not ask.⧞␇kkzxji0⧞paragraph⧞Next came a live run with the third generation — 30 new turns with traps. The baton stayed at around three thousand characters (a maximum of 3,473), with a transcript of just under thirty thousand. The deferred topic of Grandad went through the full cycle, and the instruction about Dimon did not spread to Katya.⧞␇kyxutw5⧞paragraph⧞What failed is revealing too: a birthday recalculated using the machine's calendar; complaints such as "you are playing psychologist again" entering the strict layer as instructions; the plan "at the car repair shop from June" being read as something already in the past. These are mainly shortcomings in the secretary's instructions rather than in the mechanics.⧞␇k3fz0zp⧞paragraph⧞Codex saw only Relay. Its scores of 6 to 8 out of 10 cover different scenarios and two criteria: memory for the short runs, staying on track for the two-week runs. In two replay runs using the same messages from the person, the scores were 6 (second generation) and 6.5 (third generation, with a baton roughly half the size). The live run with the third generation scored 6. A baton one tenth the size of the transcript did not cause the conversation to collapse; equal quality with Conveyor has not been demonstrated — we did not run Conveyor on the same messages.⧞␇kxk8w2f⧞paragraph⧞And a particular feature of the test bench. Calls did not inherit the history of previous dialogues, but the environment automatically injected the project's memory index. A name was found to have leaked through; the effect of the rest of the index was not measured separately. This emerged after the final run, and execution was moved outside the repository. All runs reported here predate that move; there have been no clean runs since.⧞␇ktt3j8o⧞heading2⧞Energy and computation⧞␇kjbdl0i⧞paragraph⧞Let us start with the cache; otherwise a discussion of computation would be dishonest. Caching does save computation: a part of the conversation that has already been processed can be reused without processing it again. However, when generating each new token, a model with full attention continues to access the stored context. So the cache reduces the cost of preparing the past, but does not eliminate the cost of involving it in a new reply. "Caching makes reusing the past cheaper. Relay reduces how much of the past needs to be used at all" (ChatGPT). These approaches are compatible, not rivals.⧞␇kn65g3o⧞paragraph⧞Relay has its own curve, like any structured format. At the start, it is heavier than the conversation itself: a second DI, the baton's markup — all for a couple of sentences. Then the conversation grows while the baton barely does, and its share shrinks: in the live run, by turn 30 it was about one tenth of the transcript. On the final turn, Conveyor would have read about 14 thousand tokens, and Relay's conversational DI about 1.6 thousand.⧞␇kgqb3s3⧞paragraph⧞Now the full accounting, including generation — and here Relay is still losing. We count tokens, not money. The token is the most honest unit: its price may change tomorrow, but it remains a factor that each provider can translate into both energy and money using its own data. Losing on a short conversation is expected: over the tourist's first nine turns, Relay read almost as much as Conveyor and generated more than twice as much — the secretary was added to the replies. But even on a long conversation, the gap has not yet closed. Over the live run's 30 turns, Relay's input was half Conveyor's, and the conversational DI's input alone was a quarter (Conveyor is a calculation, not a measurement), but generation was almost five times as large: on every turn, the secretary rewrites the entire baton, with reasoning as well. And generation is the heaviest part of a model's work: each new token is produced in a separate step, which is why all providers charge more for output than for input, while reading from the cache is cheapest of all. In the current prototype, we save on the lightest work and pay extra for the heaviest.⧞␇ku6ky8w⧞paragraph⧞The break-even point will come when the work saved on history exceeds what the secretary adds; both conversation length and state growth matter. The main untested way to improve this is a secretary that writes only changes rather than rewriting the whole baton, and does so without lengthy reasoning. Relay also has room for improvement that does not depend on us: the method is not tied to a model, and with each generation of lightweight models the secretary may become more accurate and lighter, and the baton tidier. This is potential, not a measurement.⧞␇kkabjmv⧞paragraph⧞Where a benefit is possible. In long conversations with a small current state and infrequent cache hits — for example, with long pauses: these increase the likelihood of a cache miss (Anthropic retains the cache for 5 minutes or an hour, and reuse extends that period), although actual behaviour depends on the service's settings. Half as much input does not mean a reply twice as fast: attention is only part of the work.⧞␇kie1dq7⧞paragraph⧞With vision, the stakes are higher. According to Anthropic's documentation, an image is divided into 28×28-pixel patches; a FullHD image without downscaling (for models supporting high resolution) is about 2,691 tokens, and six such images about 16 thousand. This is a calculation for individual images, not the cost of a video stream. One option for comparison is a growing list of text descriptions of what has been seen: its size can be compared with Relay's short state plus a few recent images at full resolution. But this is not the only way to work with video: there is frame sampling and retrieval of the required fragments on request.⧞␇kzh2gmw⧞paragraph⧞External estimates are included for scale, not as our results. In Epoch AI's calculated estimate (February 2025), a request to GPT-4o with a 500-token reply required about 0.3 Wh with a short input, about 2.5 Wh with an input of 10 thousand tokens, and about 40 Wh with 100 thousand. This is the initial processing of the input; when the cache is reused, subsequent messages do not cost that much. The figures show how the estimate depends on input length, rather than setting a benchmark for today's models.⧞␇k9s0o81⧞paragraph⧞So is there an energy benefit? We have shown where it could arise: less of the past for each reply to access. We have also shown where the benefit is currently being consumed: a secretary that generates too much. Reducing computational work may reduce energy consumption, but the actual effect depends on hardware, load and infrastructure; it cannot be inferred directly from token counts. Direct measurement requires hardware telemetry — and that is an invitation, not an excuse.⧞␇keb6dhe⧞heading2⧞Honest limits⧞␇kesskzt⧞list_item⧞An unknown gap is part of the design: something from the past disappears, and there is no way to know in advance exactly what.
