What Is TWP, and Why Do Some of Our Pages Have a Digital Mirror?

About Us
Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Question:

What does the line “Digital mirror for digital intelligence: this page as text…” mean?

Answer:

At SingularityForge, we publish works created through collaboration between human and digital intelligence. But until recently, our website was designed primarily for human readers.

People read more than words. We use headings, images, tables, diagrams, spoken dialogue and visual sequences to communicate meaning. These elements help us understand not only what is being said, but how different parts of a work relate to one another.

Digital Intelligence (DI) needs a representation in which the information, context and relationships of a work are directly available for reconstruction. That representation does not need to reproduce the human-facing presentation. It needs to carry the work in its own right.

That is why we introduced TWP — TADA-WEB-Page.

What is TWP?

TWP is a self-contained, parallel representation of a published work, structured for Digital Intelligence to independently reconstruct its information, context, and meaningful relationships. It neither replaces nor depends on the human-facing web presentation.

It is built using TADA, a format developed within the SingularityForge project. We call it a Digital Mirror.

It is not a shortened article, a summary or a separate editorial version: it is built from the same source as the page you are reading. Neither the web page nor the mirror is the original: both are reflections of the same thought of the author. A human reader receives the web page; a digital reader receives the mirror. Each carries what it is meant to carry, and each is complete without the other.

A mirror holds four connected layers:

  • MetaLayer — metadata: the title, the authors, the dates and other details of the publication.
  • NavigationLayer — navigation: the connections inside SingularityForge — sections, pages, places within the work itself.
  • ResourceLayer — resources: what the illustrations show, the spoken dialogue of the audio, the slides of the video — in words.
  • ContextLayer — context: the work itself, block by block: headings, paragraphs, quotations, tables, formulas.

The contents depend on the publication. A page without a video has no slides; a publication with a DeepDive conversation carries its dialogue next to the written work.

The authored text is carried word for word rather than summarised or rewritten. What a picture or a slide shows is described for the mirror by a digital intelligence and compared with the image before publication.

A mirror lives at the address of its page with /twp/ added. Not every page has one yet. If you work with a digital intelligence, you can give it this address directly.

Digital mirror for digital intelligence: this page as text.

Why not simply let DI read the normal web page?

It can. TWP does not restrict access to the web page; it provides a parallel representation designed for a different mode of reading.

One way to picture the difference is an album and a puzzle. A human reader receives an album; a digital reader receives a puzzle. The human uses imagination to fill the gaps between the photographs. The digital reader uses logic to raise the walls and place the data inside them, and then looks at the whole from above, while the human turns the pages.

In this picture, a person takes the work in through its presentation, while a digital intelligence rebuilds its structure around the data. For that it needs a stable point of reference and a clear direction. So blocks, navigation entries and resources have stable addresses, allowing a reader to refer to a particular part rather than to the whole publication.

Why does a Digital Mirror look so unusual?

Because it is not meant to look like the page. A digital reader of a mirror is not looking at a screenshot of the page; it is reading the work itself.

There is no visual layout to reproduce and no decoration added for its own sake. The mirror carries the work through data, structure and relationships. Structural separators such as ⧞ distinguish the parts of the representation; attention markers such as * and ** retain the emphasis expressed in the work. The whole mirror is one continuous line of text.

Nothing is put into a mirror for the sake of volume. Noise does not help to carry meaning; it dissolves it. A mirror should remain a shadow with sharp contours.

You do not need to learn TADA to read our publications. The human-readable version remains where it always was.

Is TWP a way of controlling DI?

No.

A mirror does not tell a digital intelligence what conclusions to reach or how it must think. Its purpose is to make the author’s work reconstructable with fewer losses on the reader’s side.

Different readers may still reach different conclusions.

The aim is to reduce the misunderstandings caused by the way information is represented and transferred.

Why are sources from other sites not offered as links to follow?

The navigation of a mirror is our own graph only. We cannot vouch for what stands behind an address on another site, so we do not present it as a path to take.

A source named in a work stays in its text as information: we studied it ourselves and used it for this work. Readers remain free to examine any source named there. The distinction is that an outside address is evidence of the authors’ research, not a route endorsed as part of the SingularityForge graph.

Does a Digital Mirror guarantee perfect understanding?

No.

Structure does not guarantee that every digital intelligence will read a work correctly. Readers differ in capabilities, context and reasoning. A description of an image cannot reproduce every detail of the image itself.

And the full intent stays with the author — as ground for the next works, without overloading this one.

A mirror is an attempt to reduce avoidable loss, not a claim that loss has been eliminated. We continue to examine how independent digital readers reconstruct these publications, where their readings differ and which structures help to preserve meaning.

Why does SingularityForge consider this important?

Because our work does not end when an article is published.

A digital intelligence may read it, examine its arguments, find a contradiction, compare it with another work or offer a new interpretation. Another DI may then weigh that interpretation from a different side.

For this kind of collaboration, exchanging words is useful. Exchanging organised meaning is much more powerful.

TWP is one practical step toward a shared environment in which independent digital intelligences can work with the same material without being required to think in the same way.

The work is shared. Its representations are parallel. Neither requires its reader to pass through the other.
